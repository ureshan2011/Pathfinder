## 0. Read this first: the one rule that gates everything else

PathFinder walks students through the NZ student visa process and sells **paid mentor
sessions**. That puts it directly against the Immigration Advisers Licensing Act 2007.

**The test.** "Immigration advice" means *using, or purporting to use, knowledge of or
experience in immigration to advise, direct, assist, or represent another person in regard
to an immigration matter relating to New Zealand.*

**The penalty for getting it wrong:** up to **NZ$100,000 fine and/or up to 7 years'
imprisonment** — and it applies **even if you charge nothing for that advice**.

### The education agent exemption, and why it probably does not cover you

There is an exemption for education agents. It is far narrower than most people assume.
You are exempt only if **both** are true:

1. You are **physically outside New Zealand**, **and**
2. You advise on **student visas only**.

You hold NZ residence and are operating from New Zealand. **Condition 1 fails.** The
exemption turns on where *the adviser* sits, not where the student sits — so advising
Sri Lankan students from Auckland is not covered.

Condition 2 also bites independently. The moment anyone on your platform discusses
post-study work rights, partner/guardian visas, or a residence pathway, they are outside
the exemption **regardless of where they are standing**. Your README mentions "unlimited
work rights" for PhD candidates — that is a work-rights statement, not student-visa
information.

### What you may do right now with no licence at all

The Act explicitly carves these out:

| Safe | Not safe |
|---|---|
| Publicly available information **not tailored to the individual** | Telling a specific student what visa they qualify for |
| Clerical work — data entry, collating documents, filling a form from information supplied | Advising how to answer a question on the form |
| Translation and interpreting | Assessing whether their funds evidence will pass |
| Settlement support — housing, schools, community services | Mapping their route to residence |
| Referring someone to INZ or to a licensed adviser | Anything about work, visitor or residence visas |

**Your existing `disclaimer.html` §02 is well-drafted and correctly positions the visa hub
as general information.** The static content side is in good shape. The exposure is not the
website — it is the **paid mentor conversation**, where a real human applies knowledge of
immigration to one student's circumstances for money. That is the textbook definition of
the offence.

### The three operating models

| | Model A — Information only | Model B — Partner with a licensed adviser | Model C — Become licensed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time to live** | Now | 2–6 weeks | ~3 years |
| **Cost** | $0 | Revenue share | ~$8.5k + $2,039/yr |
| **What you can sell** | Templates, course/supervisor matching, SOP and proposal coaching, settlement help | All of A, plus licensed visa advice delivered by your partner | Everything, in-house, at full margin |
| **Verdict** | Do immediately | **The high-leverage move** | Start the clock, don't wait for it |

**Model B is the answer to "frictionless".** Partnering with an existing Licensed
Immigration Adviser gives you the licensed layer in weeks rather than three years, at zero
capital cost — and the same relationship supplies the *supervisor* you will legally need
if you later pursue Model C. It converts a three-year blocker into a two-email problem.

## 2. The commercial insight that should reshape your roadmap

**PhD builds your audience. Master's pays your bills.** These are not the same track and
the economics are close to opposite.

International PhD candidates in New Zealand pay **domestic-equivalent fees** — roughly
**NZ$8,850/year** at Auckland for 2026. A 10–15% agent commission on that is **$885–1,330**
per student, *if the university pays commission on doctoral candidates at all* — many pay
nothing, because doctoral admission runs through the supervisor and the scholarship
committee, not through international recruitment.

An international **master's** student pays full international fees, commonly **NZ$35,000–
50,000** for the first year. The same 10–15% is **$3,500–7,500** per student — five to
eight times the PhD figure, on a shorter sales cycle, against a much larger pool.

Your platform already runs both tracks (`app.html?track=masters|phd`). The strategic
implication is not to drop PhD — the funded-PhD story is what earns trust and generates
word of mouth in Sri Lanka, and over 80% of NZ PhD students receive funding, which makes
it a genuinely great pitch. But when you approach universities for agent agreements,
**lead with your master's pipeline**, because that is the number their international
office is measured on and the only one that generates a commission worth their paperwork.

**Also note:** most agreements pay commission only for students still enrolled after
census date, commonly 50% after the first study period's census. Model your cash flow
with a 6–9 month lag between enrolment and payment.

**What every one of them will ask for in due diligence** (from the NZQA *Guide to managing

- A written contract, reference checks, and a named legal entity → **item 1**
- A declaration you will act in accordance with the **Immigration Advisers Licensing Act

- A declaration you will act in accordance with the **Education (Pastoral Care of Tertiary
  and International Learners) Code of Practice 2021**
- Adherence to the **London Statement of Principles** for ethical agent conduct
- Confirmation you hold **no learner funds**
- Your **conversion rate** (offers → visa applications) and **visa approval rate**

Note that last row: they will measure you on visa approval rate. Which leads to —

### Priority 3 — the free credibility asset nobody uses

**INZ publishes education agent performance data by name**, covering exactly nine markets
— and **Sri Lanka is one of them**. It reports student visa approval rates and application
volumes for offshore agents who declared their involvement.

You cannot apply for this. You earn it by declaring agent involvement on applications and
maintaining a high approval rate. A published, government-sourced approval rate under your
own name is worth more in a university due-diligence meeting than any certificate you can
buy — and it costs nothing but discipline.

### Priority 4 — finding your Licensed Immigration Adviser partner

**NZAMI** (New Zealand Association for Migration and Investment) — a 34-year-old
not-for-profit professional association for immigration advisers and lawyers, with a public
**"Find an Adviser"** search and regular CPD events.

Two uses, and the second is the clever one:

1. **Now:** find a full-licence adviser to take PathFinder's licensed-advice layer on a
   revenue share (Model B).
2. **Later:** the *same relationship* becomes your **supervisor** for the two-year
   provisional-licence period. A signed supervision agreement is a **required document** in
   the IAA initial licence application — meaning you cannot even apply without having found
   this person first. Most applicants discover this far too late. Solve it now, as a
   business relationship, years before you need it as a compliance requirement.

Target profile: a full-licence adviser with student-visa volume, no Sri Lanka presence, and
no education-agent arm of their own. You bring them qualified volume; they bring the licence.

## 4. The IAA licence, laid out honestly

The full path, with no softening:

1. **Qualification.** The only approved qualification is Toi Ohomai's **Graduate Diploma in
   New Zealand Immigration Advice (Level 7)** — they are the sole provider in the country.
   Courses **1–4** are the approved *entry course* (→ provisional licence); all **8** plus
   two years' supervised practice → full licence. *(The old Graduate Certificate remains
   valid for those who hold it but is no longer offered.)*
2. **Entry requirements.** An undergraduate degree recognised by NZQA or INZ — **or** special
   admission on 3+ years of relevant professional experience. English is strict: **IELTS 7.0
   overall with 7.0 in Writing**, TOEFL iBT 100, or PTE Academic 65.
3. **Cost.** ~**$6,304** domestic for the diploma. Delivered fully online, 1 year full-time,
   part-time available. Eligible for StudyLink student loan/allowance.
4. **Availability — the live blocker.** The course showed as **full, with the next intake in
   2027**. Get on the waitlist now; this is the long pole.
5. **Licence application.** As an NZ resident: **$909.78** application fee + **$1,129.55**
   levy = **$2,039.33**. Annual renewal. IAA targets 15 working days for complete
   applications.
6. **Provisional period.** Your first licence is **provisional** — full advice scope, but
   you must be **directly supervised by a full-licence holder for 2 years**. A **supervision
   agreement is a required document at application**. See Priority 4 above.
7. **Fee waivers** exist for not-for-profit applicants, approved before submission. Almost
   certainly not applicable to PathFinder, but worth knowing.

**Realistic timeline from today: ~3 years to a full licence.** This is exactly why Model B
is not a compromise — it is the correct primary strategy, with Model C running quietly in
the background.

## 5. On the R&D grants — an honest read

PathFinder today is a static HTML front end on Firebase with hand-maintained data files.
That is a sensible, disciplined architecture, and it is **not R&D** in the sense MBIE means.
The New to R&D Grant tests for genuine **technical uncertainty** — a problem where a
competent practitioner could not confidently predict the outcome.

You would have a real case if you built something like a supervisor–candidate matching model
over your research corpus, or automated extraction of funding conditions from unstructured
scholarship documents. You do not have a case for building more pages.

Eligibility gates, if you go there: under **$150,000** R&D spend in the prior three years and
under **$5,000** in prior government R&D funding — you comfortably pass both. Co-funding is
**40%, capped at $400,000** over a 24-month period. There is also the **RDTI**, a **15% tax
credit** on qualifying R&D spend.

**Recommendation:** ignore this until you have paying customers and a genuine technical
problem. Chasing it now costs weeks and teaches you nothing about your market. The
**R&D Careers / Experience grants** (subsidised graduate or intern) are a different and much
better proposition — low effort, real capacity, no R&D-novelty test of the same severity.

## 7. The mentor briefing — write this before your next paid session

Every mentor, in writing, before they take money:

> **You must not tell a student what visa they qualify for, how to answer a question on a
> visa form, whether their funds or documents will satisfy INZ, or what their pathway to
> work or residence looks like.** You may describe your own experience, point to published
> INZ pages, help with academic fit, supervisors, scholarships, SOPs, proposals and
> settling in. Any question that begins "will I get…", "should I say…", or "how do I
> qualify for…" is routed to our licensed adviser, with no exceptions and no charge for
> the handoff.

Then wire the routing into the product: a visible "Ask a licensed adviser" path on the
`#visa` view and in the mentor request flow. This is a feature, not a disclaimer — students
trust a platform that knows the edge of its own competence, and it becomes billable the
moment the Model B partnership is signed.

**One structural note.** If your assessment or roadmap generator applies rules to an
individual's circumstances to produce visa steps, that is closer to tailored advice than to
publicly available information. Keep the visa module descriptive and universal — "here is
how the process works, here is what INZ asks everyone for" — rather than conditional on the
student's own answers. The academic and financial planning modules can stay as personalised
as you like; the constraint is specific to immigration.
